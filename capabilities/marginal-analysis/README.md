# Marginal Analysis

Marginal cost, marginal revenue, and the output decision that follows from them — where the cost of
one more unit crosses what that unit sells for, and what stops production when it does.

**Exercised in:** perfect-competition — a 1.5-acre market garden choosing how many beds of
tomatoes, carrots, and mesclun to plant for a 36-week season. Diminishing returns raise the labor
cost of each additional bed, so marginal cost rises and the price-taker's P = MC rule sets the mix.
The model commits the season to 10 tomato beds, 20 carrot, and 30 mesclun — $42,761.66 of profit on
60 of the 64 beds. Tomatoes stop where marginal cost crosses the $8,800 price between beds 10 and
11; carrots and mesclun stop at their bed caps instead, still $405.05 and $279.90 under price, and
worth $352.49 and $246.47 a bed to relax. Land and temporary labor never bind.
